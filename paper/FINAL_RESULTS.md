### The two comparisons the paper is allowed to make

1. **Against Random (the uninformed floor):** 0.625 -> 0.792, while motion cost falls from 4.00 m to 2.56 m (-36%).
   *Consequence planning beats uninformed stochastic exploration while using less motion.*

2. **Against the classical deterministic policies:** 0.250 -> 0.792.
   *Action-conditioned consequence prediction substantially outperforms fixed and reactive strategies.*

**Forbidden claim.** `0.250 -> 0.792` must **not** be presented as the headline improvement, because Random reaches 0.625 and thus sits *above* every classical rule. The classical rules are deterministic commitment policies: they repeat the same failing action until the budget expires. That is why Random beats them, and it must be stated.

### Wilson 95% intervals at RSR@10

- Reactive: RSR@10 = 0.250
- HistoryOnly: RSR@10 = 0.250
- NBV: RSR@10 = 0.250
- Oracle: RSR@10 = 0.875
- OracleSearch: RSR@10 = 1.000
- 24 terrains; Random is a mean over 3 seeds and its interval is the spread across seeds, not a Wilson interval.

## Two validation checks - different sets, different questions

These must never be conflated. They are reported over different collections and answer different questions, and mixing them invites the obvious question of why one number is short of the other.

| Check | Set | Question | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solvability | **24 benchmark scenarios** | does a successful action sequence exist? | **24/24 solvable**, upper bound 1.00 |
| Terrain check | **15 terrain-kit primitives** | is the geometry self-consistent and the terrain walkable? | **14/15 pass** (12 walked, 14 geometry-consistent) |

The terrain kit includes primitives that are **not** benchmark scenarios (`stairs_down`, `window_wall`, `flat`, `crater`, `undulating`). The single failure is the legacy `undulating` height-field terrain, which is excluded from the benchmark suite. So `14/15` concerns terrain primitives and `24/24` concerns task solvability; neither contradicts the other.
